abstract: "The 'synchronized' keyword is frequently used in multithreaded Java applications to prevent data races and thus produce intended results. However, it works by implementing a lock on a resource, and when a thread uses that resource, it becomes locked to other threads. Not only does implementing this lock have significant overhead, it also causes threads to wait for long periods of time and is thus a major efficiency bottleneck. In the context of applications where efficiency is far more important than accuracy, such as the context of GDI in this assignment, unsafe alternatives are worth exploring. Here, I explore two alternatives to synchronized: one that uses the volatile keyword to ensure a visibility guarantee (AcmeSafeState) and another that puts no restrictions on threads/memory accesses at all (Unsynchronized state). Results reveal that when using few threads, it is better to use unsynchronized or other unsafe methods. However, when using many threads, the efficiency difference between synchronized and unsynchronized is very little, and it would be better to use the synchronized method so as to guarantee perfect results."
