title: "Adoption Is Not Security: Measuring BGP Route Protection"
publishedDate: 2026-08-13
category: "cybersecurity"
abstract: "The Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) is the standard method of routing requests through the internet, but is vulnerable to prefix hijacking, where requests to a particular address may be routed toward the wrong destination. To mitigate this risk, the Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) introduced the Resource Public Key Infrastructure (RPKI) in 2011 @rfc8210. However, a significant portion of autonomous systems (ASes) have not adopted RPKI's route origin authentication (ROA) or validation (ROV) @ROVista @RPKIage. In this work, we evaluate the security of routes between ASes by using public datasets to construct a graph of AS routes, and traversing this graph using a bidirectional breadth-first search that satisfies the Gao-Rexford model. We searched for valid paths between two sets of AS pairs and categorized these paths based on ROV adoption of all ASes in the path, and the destination AS's ROA adoption. We find that 56.8% of routes between stub ASes and major transit providers are secure, but this drops to 14.7% for randomly sampled AS pairs, revealing that RPKI deployment is heavily concentrated among large networks and that aggregate adoption statistics substantially overstate the Internet's actual routing security."
